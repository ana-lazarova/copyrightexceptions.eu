title: "Section 29 of the Copyright Law"
date: 2022
draft: false
weight: 14
exceptions:
- dsm8
jurisdictions:
- CY
score: 3
description: "This exception allows cultural heritage institutions or organisations to use works or other protected subject matter that are not available in commerce and are permanently in their collections, for non-commercial purposes, to be made available on non-commercial websites." 
beneficiaries:
- cultural heritage institutions or organisations
purposes: 
- non-commercial
usage:
- reproduction
- making available to the public
- extraction and re-use (of sui generis right databases)
subjectmatter:
- works (incl. computer programmes)
- databases
- phonograms
- film fixations
- broadcasts
- publications of previously unpublished works
- performances
- press publications
compensation:
- no compensation required
attribution: 
- the name of the author or any other identifiable rightholder must be mentioned, unless established impossible
otherConditions: 
- the exceptions only applies to types of works or other subject matter for which no 'sufficiently representative' collective management organisation exists
- rightholders may at any time exclude their works or other subject-matter from the application of the exception either generally or in specific cases, including cases following the commencement of the relevant use
remarks: "According to §29(5), an work is considered unavailable in commerce when it can be concluded, by evidence, in good faith that the entire work or other subject matter is not available to the public through ordinary channels of commerce, after a reasonable effort has been made to determine whether it is available to the public.<br /><br/>In terms of subject matter, the approach to the implementation of the CDSM Directive differs from the referencing scheme of the pre-existing E&Ls system, where there is one main exception applying to 'intellectual property' (meaning works, incl. computer programmes, but also sui generis right databases, phonograms, broadcasts and publications of previously unpublished works) and a reference to related rights (meaning press publications and performances). Here, the provision of §29 covers 'works and other protected subject matter'. In addition, according to §44(3), the provisions of §§ 24 to 43 apply to all works and other subject matter protected by national copyright law on or after the date of entry into force of the provisions of this Law. At the same time, §29 explicitly refers to different types of usage for different subject matter."
