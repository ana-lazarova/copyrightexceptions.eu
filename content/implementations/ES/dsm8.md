description: "This exception allows cultural heritage institutions to reproduce, distribute, communicate to the public, or make available to the public, for non-commercial purposes, out-of-commerce works or other subject matter that are permanently held in the institutions' collections. It also includes 'total or partial reproduction, by any means and in any form, whether permanent or temporary'. It also covers transformation of the work and reproduction of the results of such transformation, without prejudice to the rights of the person carrying out the transformation. Reproduction for the purpose of distribution or public communication (other than making available to the public) of the work requires explicit authorisation. For the exception to kick in, there should not be a sufficiently representative collective management organisation." 
beneficiaries:
- cultural heritage institutions
purposes: 
- non-commercial
usage:
- reproduction (unless necessary for distribution or CTTP)
- distribution (any form)
- communication to the public (on non-commercial websites)
- making available (on non-commercial websites)
- transformation
subjectmatter:
- works
- databases
- other subject matter
compensation:
- no compensation required
attribution: 
- the name of the author or any other identifiable rightsholder must be indicated, whenever possible
otherConditions: 
- works must be permanently held in the institutions' collections (except for databases, publicly available databases being covered as well)
- for the exception to kick in, there should not be a sufficiently representative collective management organisation
- where the distribution or communication to the public of the work or subject matter requires reproduction, authorisation must be obtained for such reproduction from the rightholder
remarks: "The exception explicitly applies to publicly available databases (para 7). It also extends to legitimate users of a database, regardless of how the latter has been disseminated (para 9). Such legitimate users can extract and/or reuse a substantial part of databases' content when making out-of-commerce works available.<br /><br />It is unclear wheter the exception extends to neighbouring rights. Art. 71 explicitly mentions works, databases and 'other subject matter'. However, the transposition of the CDSM Directive was effected by Royal Decree-Law 24/2021, enacted as a standalone legislative text functioning in parallel to the Intellectual Property Law. As a result, the general rule laid down in art. 132 of the Intellectual Property Law, which provides for the horizontal application of exceptions to all neighbouring rights, does not apply.<br /><br />There appears to be no option for the author to opt out of the use."
